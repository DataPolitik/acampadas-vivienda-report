A [DataPolitik](https://www.heuristica.barcelona/datapolitik/) study by [Heurística](https://www.heuristica.barcelona/), Barcelona.

A study of the public Telegram channels of the Spanish housing movement and their
allies during the housing camps that followed the eviction of Maricarmen Abascal in
Madrid, and in the run-up to the general strike for housing called for 11 November:
how the channels were
sampled and classified, and what their forwarding and mention networks show.

Blocks 1 onwards extend the first study in weekly blocks, scanning the same channels
in every block and adding those that enter the conversation. How they work, and how

Each observation period introduces new data and analyses. Previous reports remain
available to document the evolution of the research, including revisions and
changes in interpretation.

## Across the periods

![Key measures from the first period to block 3](figures/followup/trajectory.png)

1. **The Madrid union stops being the hub.** The Sindicato de Inquilinas de Madrid is
   forwarded by 14 channels in the first study and 10 in block 1, after Maricarmen's
   death, then by 1 and 2 in blocks 2 and 3.
2. **Málaga's strike committee channel carries the forwarding of housing content** from
   block 2, but through the same three or four channels: volume without breadth.
3. **The mobilisation turns from the camps to the 11 November strike,** and channels that
   back the strike while disputing its organisers grow from 5 to 13 in a week.
4. **Territorial housing clusters do not forward each other** in any period; the first
   tie across territories, in block 3, is a joint statement by the camps after the Urda
   rail crash.
5. **The camps' own channels are barely relayed** in any period.
6. **Two hostile media channels form a closed pair** that no other channel forwards or
   quotes (recorded from block 1, when the opponent category was introduced).

## About this site

This repository brings together successive results from ongoing research into
Telegram communication networks surrounding housing mobilisations in Spain. It
publishes reports, visualisations, methodological documentation, classification
decisions and the processed evidence behind the analyses, so that the research
process can be followed, the findings examined critically, and the evolution of the
interpretations documented as new observations become available.

The findings are provisional, subject to revision, and do not aim to provide an
exhaustive representation of the movement.

- Only public Telegram channels and groups were read, without joining them.
- Collection, classification and analysis were carried out by an AI agent following a
  written protocol, with the team setting the protocol, taking the decisions that changed
  the sample or the classification, and reviewing the results.
- Code and raw data are not published.
- In the evidence files, individual user accounts and hidden-name forward origins are
  pseudonymised, and the text of members' messages in group chats is removed. Channels
  are identified by their public username.
- The sample is a snowball from a nine-channel seed, not a census. See the limitations
  sections before drawing conclusions.

**Feedback, discussion, and use of the findings**

This repository is open to critical examination, discussion, and reuse of its findings.
We particularly invite collectives, unions, and activists to take ownership of the
analyses, interpret them in light of their own experiences, and use them as they see fit.

If you have questions, would like to discuss interpretations, point out errors, or share
other uses and perspectives, we would be happy to hear from you: open an
[issue in the repository](https://github.com/DataPolitik/acampadas-vivienda-report/issues) or write through
[Heurística's contact page](https://www.heuristica.barcelona/contactheu/).
